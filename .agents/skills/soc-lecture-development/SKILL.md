# SOC Lecture Development

## Purpose

Develop clear, progressive, technically accurate lectures that help learners understand concepts and apply them in SOC work.

## Before starting

- Read the target module overview, learning outcomes, prerequisites, and existing lecture assets.
- Read applicable curriculum, instructional quality, documentation, and visual-content instructions.
- Inspect canonical templates in `templates/` if present; do not invent a competing house format.
- Confirm learner level, lecture duration, delivery mode, and requested assets. Ask one focused question if a missing detail blocks the work.

## Method

1. Convert module outcomes into lecture-level measurable outcomes.
2. Build a teaching sequence from foundational concepts to operational application.
3. For each topic, explain the concept, why it matters to a SOC analyst, relevant telemetry, investigation use, and common misconceptions.
4. Add demonstrations or short activities only where they reinforce an outcome.
5. Separate student-facing explanations from instructor notes, expected answers, and delivery cues.
6. Add knowledge checks and map them to the outcomes.
7. Identify where a lab, scenario, or assessment should provide deeper practice instead of overloading the lecture.
8. Cite authoritative technical references and record version-sensitive assumptions.
9. Check consistency with prior and subsequent modules.

## Required outputs

As applicable:
- Lecture brief and learning outcomes
- Teaching agenda and topic sequence
- Student learning content
- Instructor notes and demonstration guidance
- Slide outline/source when requested
- Knowledge checks and answer guidance
- References and follow-on practical activities

## Quality checks

- The lecture fits the stated duration and learner level.
- Technical terms are defined before use.
- Explanations are accurate, current, and not vendor-dependent without reason.
- Demonstrations have prerequisites and expected observations.
- Instructor-only material is clearly separated.
- Activities and questions align with stated outcomes.
- References support the claims they accompany.

## Handoff

List files, outcomes covered, references, assumptions, checks performed, and remaining gaps. Mark draft/in-review; request independent review rather than self-approval.
