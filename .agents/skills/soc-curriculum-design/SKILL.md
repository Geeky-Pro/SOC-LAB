# SOC Curriculum Design

## Purpose

Create a coherent, measurable SOC learning pathway that develops operational capability progressively and aligns teaching, practice, and assessment.

## Before starting

- Read the Project Charter, curriculum architecture, approved module list, content lifecycle, and instructional quality standard.
- Inspect existing curriculum documents and the lab dependency map before proposing changes.
- Confirm whether the request is a local module adjustment or a curriculum-level change.
- Treat scope, module order, and approved outcomes as controlled decisions; escalate proposed changes to the Project Owner.

## Method

1. Define target learners, entry level, prerequisites, delivery constraints, and intended capability at completion.
2. Express outcomes with observable action verbs and measurable evidence.
3. Group outcomes into coherent capability areas; avoid organizing the curriculum solely around tools.
4. Sequence modules by prerequisite knowledge and increasing investigative complexity.
5. Map each outcome to teaching topics, practical activities, scenario evidence, and assessment methods.
6. Identify shared lab dependencies, telemetry dependencies, and concepts that must be introduced earlier.
7. Check workload, repetition, gaps, and unnecessary scope.
8. Record assumptions, dependencies, and decisions requiring owner approval.

## Required outputs

As appropriate to the request:
- Curriculum overview and rationale
- Module sequence with purpose and outcomes
- Learning-outcome/assessment mapping
- Competency matrix
- Prerequisite and dependency map
- Scope gaps, overlaps, and owner decisions

## Quality checks

- Outcomes are measurable and learner-centered.
- Module order reflects real prerequisites.
- Each outcome has a teaching and assessment path.
- Practical outcomes are supported by available or planned lab capabilities.
- Tool coverage supports competencies rather than driving the curriculum.
- Out-of-scope areas are not introduced implicitly.
- No unapproved scope or architecture decision is presented as final.

## Handoff

State what changed, why, affected outcomes/modules, dependencies, assumptions, and decisions required. Keep the deliverable in draft/in-review pending independent review and Project Owner approval.
