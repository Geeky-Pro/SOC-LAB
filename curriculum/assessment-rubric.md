# SOC-LAB Assessment Rubric

> **Status:** draft — common performance descriptors for module and capstone assessment.
> **Authority:** Project Owner approval required before operational use.
> **Scoring policy:** No weighting, pass threshold, or grade boundary is implied.

## Performance levels
| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 4 — Strong | Accurate, complete, independently reasoned, reproducible, and communicates limitations clearly. |
| 3 — Competent | Meets the outcome with sound reasoning and only minor omissions that do not change the conclusion. |
| 2 — Developing | Partial understanding; material omissions, weak corroboration, or requires instructor prompts. |
| 1 — Insufficient | Unsupported, materially inaccurate, unsafe, incomplete, or not reproducible from submitted evidence. |
| N/O — Not observed | The task did not fairly expose this criterion; apply only according to a published assessment rule. |

## Common criteria
| Criterion | Strong (4) | Competent (3) | Developing (2) | Insufficient (1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope and safety | Defines authorized assets, time window, boundaries, stop/rollback conditions; follows them | Scope is clear and controls followed | Minor ambiguity or prompting needed | Exceeds scope, ignores controls, or acts without authorization |
| Evidence provenance | Every material claim traces to source/artifact and relevant timestamp/locator | Most claims traceable with minor gaps | Several claims lack precise references | Evidence cannot be located or provenance misrepresented |
| Technical interpretation | Correctly interprets relevant fields/protocol/behavior and explains limits | Main interpretation correct with minor omissions | Partial or overgeneralized interpretation | Materially incorrect or unsupported |
| Hypothesis testing | Tests competing explanations and records confirming/disconfirming evidence | Tests a reasonable hypothesis and considers alternatives | Limited testing | Guess presented as conclusion or contradictions ignored |
| Correlation and timeline | Normalizes time, links entities across sources, explains uncertainty/clock issues | Timeline and key correlations mostly sound | Gaps or weak source linkage | Misleading timeline or unsupported correlation |
| Prioritization and response | Proportionate actions with authority, impact, rollback, verification | Reasonable recommendation within authority | Omits material trade-offs or verification | Unsafe/unauthorized recommendation |
| Communication and records | Clear audience-appropriate report separating facts, hypotheses, unknowns, decisions | Clear report with minor omissions | Difficult to follow or mixes facts/assumptions | Misleading or incomplete |
| Validation and limitations | Separates execution, telemetry, parsing, detection, alerting, prevention, and validation states | Most states/limits distinguished | Ambiguous validation claims | Claims execution/coverage without evidence |

## Evidence minimums
As relevant, submissions identify assessment ID/date/environment or dataset, authorized scope, source/artifact references, timestamps and timezone, observed facts, hypotheses, alternatives, unknowns, reasoning, confidence, recommended action, authority/impact/rollback/verification, and whether evidence is captured, synthetic, simulated, inferred, or runtime-validated.

## Assessment use
- Map criteria to the specific outcomes being assessed; do not score irrelevant criteria.
- Identify individual and team-produced artifacts. Team evidence alone does not establish individual competence.
- Give students the brief, permitted resources, time, submission format, and rubric before assessment.
- Keep instructor keys separate from student materials.
- Project Owner decides weighting, pass threshold, retake/remediation, accommodations, and evidence retention.
